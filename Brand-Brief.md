# Brand Brief: Foldwell

For uploading to Claude Design. The app is named Foldwell (chosen October 2026; the earlier working name "Passing Notes" was taken).

## What it is
A family app where parents and kids trade real notes, voice messages, stickers, and drawings, and turn them into a printed keepsake. Parent is the account holder. Children are ages 4 to teen.

## Who it is for
- Primary user and buyer: parents and caregivers (busy, loving, wary of screens and of data collection).
- Children: ages 4–8 (cannot read well; need big, simple, voice-first), ages 9–12, and teens (will reject anything babyish or surveillance-like).

## Brand personality
Warm, calm, tactile, honest, private. Like a handwritten note in a lunchbox: personal, imperfect, loved.

Mood words: paper, handwritten, cozy, kind, quiet, personal, keepsake.

Not: loud, neon, corporate, clinical, gamey, glossy, babyish for older ages.

## Three looks, one family (chosen by age; young people can switch)

- **Ages 4-8, the Note Box:** a cardboard-style box of colorful envelopes. Big shapes, big type, big buttons, a play button for the parent's voice. Playful but calm.
- **Tweens and younger teens (about 9-13), Soft Collage (look B):** pastel sticky notes, tape, Polaroid-style photos of handwritten notes, a floating pill toolbar. Warm, a little playful, not babyish.
- **Older teens (about 14+), Oat and Ink (look A):** oat paper, dot grid, thin ink borders, monospace labels, muted sage and clay accents, small icon toolbar. Calm, editorial, and grown-up.
- All looks sit inside the same notebook idea (marbled cover, notes, stickers, doodles) and share the same layout and features. Only the styling changes.
- The look is a **theme**. Default is chosen by age band, and the young person can switch (also dark mode as an option). Build it as a flexible theme system, not three separate apps.

## Typography
- Notes and headings: a warm serif with a handwritten feeling (placeholder: Georgia).
- Controls and body: a friendly, readable sans-serif.
- Large sizes for ages 4–8. Final fonts to be chosen; use free or properly licensed fonts only.

## Shape and feel
- Rounded corners, soft shadows only where objects overlap (post-its on paper).
- Flat colors, no gradients, no glossy effects.
- Slight rotation on post-its for a handmade feel.

## Stickers and doodles
- Set A (ages 4–8): 12 big feeling stickers (love, happy, silly, proud, hug, sleepy, brave, thankful, shy, sad, worried, excited).
- Set B (ages 9+): word stickers ("same," "me too," "thank you," "I needed this," "love you," "sorry," "I'm okay," "I need a minute," "not now," "can we talk later?").
- Final art will be commissioned. For prototypes, use simple placeholders.

## Must-follow rules for any design
- No streaks, points, badges, counts, or "seen" markers.
- No red alert colors or guilt messages. Missing a day shows nothing.
- No ads or purchase buttons anywhere a child can reach.
- Children never type in the app. They only tap stickers and draw.
- Big touch targets (at least 56 points) for ages 4–8, labelled for screen readers, and respect reduced-motion settings.
- Bilingual-ready (English and Spanish); leave room for longer Spanish text.
- Thumb-zone friendly: main controls at the bottom.

## Do not
- Do not use any existing characters, brands, or copyrighted imagery.
- Do not make anything that looks like a therapy or medical app.
- Do not use words like "first," "only," or "best."

## Screens to prototype first
1. Note Box (ages 4–8): box, envelopes, open note with play button, sticker row, doodle entry.
2. Notebook (ages 9+): cover, page with post-its and a folded note, reply with sticker.
3. Parent screen: write a note, record voice, print a card.

## Tween and teen look: design notes (draft guidance, not tested with young people)

- **Light by default.** A vulnerability space should not feel heavy. Offer dark mode as an option.
- **Look B (tweens and younger teens, about 9-13):** pastel sticky notes, tape, Polaroid-style photos, floating pill toolbar, slightly playful.
- **Look A (older teens, about 14+):** editorial and understated: oat paper, dot grid, ink lines, monospace labels, muted sage and clay, small icon toolbar.
- **Their own journal, not a kids' app.** Teens reject anything that feels made for younger children.
- **Quiet language.** Lowercase labels, no exclamation marks, no cutesy copy.
- **Control and privacy are visible.** Replies are optional; stickers like "not now" and "can we talk later?" make it easy to say no.
- **Personalization.** Let the young person pick their cover, accent color, and stickers, and switch between looks. Ownership matters more than any single look.
- **Age is only a starting point.** Some 12-year-olds will want look A and some 16-year-olds will want look B.
- **Avoid chasing trends.** Build a flexible theme system (colors, cover, stickers, layout details).
- **Test with real young people** before locking either look.

Look B palette: pale sage-gray #F2F5F1, butter #F7EBC4, clay #E7B9A6, powder blue #B9CFE0, white #FFFFFF, ink #2C2C2A.
Look A palette: oat #F4EFE6, ink #2C2C2A, sage #A8C3B1, pale sage #E8EFE9, dot grid #D4CCBB, paper #FFFDF8.
